Who Orders Online? Understanding Online Food-Ordering Behavior

Main question: How does online food-ordering behavior vary across income, occupation, and family size?

85.5%
No Income has the highest online-ordering rate in this dataset. This is an association, not evidence that income causes ordering behavior.

1. Online-ordering rate by Monthly Income

Ordering rates generally decrease across higher-income groups; No Income is highest at 85.5%.

2. Online-ordering rate by Family Size

Ordering is highest for 4-person families (86.7%) and lowest for 6-person families (60.9%).

3. Online-ordering rate by Occupation

Ordering rates differ across occupations; sample size matters, especially for the small Housewife group.

4. Monthly Income × Occupation

The high No Income rate is strongly associated with its student-heavy composition; very small subgroups should not be over-interpreted.

Data & methodology: Rates are calculated as the percentage of records with Output = Yes within each displayed group. Analysis uses 285 cleaned records after removing exact duplicates and the duplicate Unnamed: 13 field, plus the specified text cleanup. Percentages in small subgroups can be unstable, so subgroup patterns are descriptive rather than causal. “No Income” is a categorical income group in the source data and should not be interpreted as zero household income.
